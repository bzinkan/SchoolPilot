# ClassPilot Teacher Guide

> Generated from the structured in-app ClassPilot guide source. Do not edit this file by hand.

Practical steps for preparing class, using classroom tools, supervising students, and finishing a session.

**Route:** /classpilot/my-settings/guide

## Prepare



### Save and choose app buttons

**Who can use this:** Teacher or administrator

Save common destinations so you can choose them quickly during class.

1. **Add a personal website.** Open Teaching tools → Website tools → My Apps and choose Add website. Enter a name and address. Your personal buttons belong to you in this school. Add website is also available in the classroom app picker.
2. **Save and favorite apps.** Browse Common apps and School apps. Add useful buttons to My Apps and use the star to put favorites first. Administrators maintain school buttons; your saved choices and favorites are personal.
3. **Choose a destination.** In Open URL or Set Focus, choosing an app fills the address. Review the recipients and destination, then confirm the action. Picking a button alone sends nothing.
4. **Add apps to a Flight Path.** In the Flight Path editor, select buttons and choose Add selected apps. Review the websites listed for each button or bundle. Docs, Slides, Sheets, and Forms can share docs.google.com access; choosing a tool button does not restrict students to that tool or one document.
5. **Review saved access.** Complete any required scope review before saving or applying a Flight Path. Editing or deleting an app button does not update websites already saved in a Flight Path. If the library fails to load, use Retry or enter the address manually.

### Prepare your class

**Who can use this:** Teacher or administrator

Check your roster and prepare reusable website tools and classroom defaults.

1. **Check class membership.** Review your assigned classes and students. Ask an administrator to correct official rosters or teacher assignments. In Teaching tools → Class setup, select a class to manage the options you are authorized to change, such as subgroups or a personal class's co-teachers.
2. **Prepare website tools.** Website tools holds your Flight Paths and Block Lists. From Classroom uses your connected Google Classroom resources to prepare a saved Flight Path; review the imported links and allowed scope before creating it. Saving a tool does not apply it to students.
3. **Use shared tools when available.** If your school has the School Library, you can apply shared Flight Paths and Block Lists from the Dashboard or choose Copy to make a private editable version. Share with school publishes an eligible personal tool to colleagues. Only administrators can change Official items.
4. **Set an optional automatic Flight Path.** In Class setup, Automatic Flight Path starts Off. The primary teacher or an administrator can choose an eligible saved path, complete any required review, and choose Save class preset. Co-teachers can view it. This is one preset for the scheduled class, and changes affect future sessions rather than the session already running.
5. **Save a personal tab default.** Under Classroom defaults, choose Use school default or a Custom limit from 1 to 100, then Save classroom defaults. This only prefills Manage Tabs. Apply a limit from the Dashboard when you want it to affect students.
6. **Check today's periods.** Open Today’s schedule to review today's class times and existing changes. Period swaps and Skip this period require your school's permission. Planned time swaps for future dates remain under Advanced.

> **Flight note:** Name saved tools for the task, such as “Period 2 Research,” so you can recognize them during class.

### Choose a dashboard theme

**Who can use this:** Teacher or administrator; school policy controls available choices

Choose a personal dashboard look when your school's appearance policy allows it.

1. **Open Appearance.** Use the Dashboard account menu or Teaching tools → Appearance. Themes may be disabled or locked to a school choice; the page explains either condition.
2. **Choose a look and save.** Choose Follow the school calendar, a preloaded theme, or Plain, no theme, then Save theme. The school calendar follows the school's holiday and season choices. A personal theme changes your dashboard, not your colleagues' dashboards or classroom browser controls.
3. **Use your own calendar.** For automatic personal changes, enable Follow the calendar: switch themes automatically by holiday and season. This uses the personal holiday calendar; Include Advent (Catholic calendar) is a separate choice. Save theme to keep the setting.
4. **Upload a background when allowed.** Choose Upload image for a PNG, JPG or WebP. Your backgrounds shows the library limit and lets you remove an image to make room. Choose the uploaded background, then Fill the page, Fit the whole picture, or Tile, and Save theme. An upload is not selected permanently until you save.
5. **Check mode and kiosk choices.** A theme replaces light or dark mode while active; Plain, no theme uses your preferred mode. Student status colors and controls keep their meaning. If you use a class-bound PassPilot kiosk, Show my theme on the PassPilot kiosk while it is bound to my class controls whether the theme also appears there.

[Open Appearance](/classpilot/my-settings?section=appearance)

### Advanced: planned time swaps

**Who can use this:** Primary teacher with school permission; approval may be required

Request an eligible future-date time swap while keeping each class's teachers and roster.

1. **Choose the right workflow.** Use Today’s schedule for changes today. Planned time swaps is the Advanced workflow for future instructional dates and earlier requests.
2. **Review an eligible pair.** Start a request, select an available class pair and date, and compare the normal and proposed times. Add a reason when school policy requires it.
3. **Send and track the request.** Use Needs Action, Upcoming, and History to follow the decision. Sending a request does not change the schedule; all required teacher acceptance and administrator approval must finish first.
4. **Refresh a changed request.** If the request was updated elsewhere, review the latest version before acting again. An approved swap affects the selected date only; regular times remain the recurring schedule.

## Start



### Start, refresh, resync, or end class

**Who can use this:** Assigned class teacher or authorized supervisor

Confirm the current assignment and distinguish a display refresh from a roster change.

1. **Confirm the assignment.** In scheduled Class mode, Class follows your server-confirmed class, testing, or coverage assignment and includes offline roster members. Wait through Starting testing or Updating class before sending tools. A reporting-only class awaiting live supervision does not grant teacher controls.
2. **Choose a class deliberately.** Choosing a class in the picker does not start it or transfer students. Use Start Class or Switch Class when offered and review the change. With no active room, the next scheduled assignment returns the Dashboard to Class. My room retains its students and chosen view across bells and reloads until you leave the view or end the room.
3. **Refresh a display or access problem.** Use Refresh class on a preview or history access warning to retry the current authorized reads. Refresh coverage serves that purpose in the earlier Claimed students view. Refreshing does not sign students in, change a frozen roster, or take students from another supervisor.
4. **Resync only a manual class.** Class actions → Resync Class is available for an active manual teaching session. It reconciles that session's roster and may ask you to review students active elsewhere before Resync Anyway. Scheduled class rosters are frozen when the occurrence starts and cannot be resynced mid-period.
5. **Review a change for today.** In Teaching tools → Today’s schedule, choose your earlier class and the class to teach first, then Review period swap. Review both teachers' four entries and choose the matching teacher if asked. Before the bell, Apply today changes the periods; during a period, the review covers only its remaining time and the later period. A teacher with an affected Live class must accept before anything changes. Regular times return tomorrow.
6. **Skip or end the named period.** Today’s schedule → a class's menu → Skip this period reviews only that period. End Class on the Dashboard reviews closing the current teaching session; ending a scheduled class early prevents that occurrence from restarting. End testing, End supervision, and End room close their named assignments instead.

### Read Dashboard status correctly

**Who can use this:** Teacher or administrator

Use connection labels, preview age, and action outcomes together.

1. **Read the student's label.** Online and Idle indicate current monitoring reports. Not logged in means no active ClassPilot student session; the preview area says Waiting for ClassPilot sign-in. The student remains on the roster and preview reads resume after sign-in.
2. **Distinguish missing reports.** Updating monitoring… is a connection transition. Monitoring signal lost means a signed-in browser has stopped reporting; it does not identify the cause. Monitoring updates unavailable means the Dashboard cannot confirm the current monitoring state.
3. **Check the preview separately.** A screenshot is a still image with its own capture time. Updating preview waits for an image matching the current control state. A retained or aging preview is not proof that commands can reach the student now.
4. **Recognize sign-in and handoffs.** Signing in can identify a supported provider sign-in flow. Monitoring handled by assigned staff means another current assignment owns the student. When a student changes session, earlier-session details are cleared before new details appear.
5. **Retry an access warning.** A student waiting for sign-in is different from a preview or history access warning. Use the warning's Refresh class or Refresh coverage action to recheck your current access, then read any remaining error.

> **Check before continuing:** An image, connection label, or device acknowledgement alone does not establish that every requested control took effect.

## Guide



### Target exact students and read results

**Who can use this:** Assigned teacher or authorized supervisor

Review the recipients before sending and keep partial outcomes visible.

1. **Choose and review recipients.** Use student checkboxes, Select All, or an available subgroup. Set Focus and Stop Focus require checkbox selection. For every action, check the displayed count and recipient names; clicking a tile opens its preview rather than selecting it.
2. **Keep an open dialog's recipients.** Message, Attention, timer, poll, Open URL, and other reviewed tools keep the recipients captured when opened. Changing the Dashboard selection does not add students to that action. If recipients become unavailable, review the explanation and explicitly choose Send to N available when offered.
3. **Separate saved from confirmed.** Restriction saved means the desired control was recorded. Pending and received outcomes still await confirmation; they do not mean enforcement is complete. A completed browser action is device-reported confirmation. Student Sign Out has a separate server-confirmed completion.
4. **Act on unsuccessful outcomes.** Read failed, unavailable, and expired results as well as successful ones. An unavailable student may need to reconnect, sign in, update ClassPilot, or return to your authority. A restriction reported as not delivered is not queued for that student; apply it again after the stated issue is resolved.
5. **Rechoose a cleared selection.** If the Dashboard clears your selection, choose recipients again. Missing or moved students never turn an action into a whole-class send. Retry only the students who still need the action.

### Open a URL and manage tabs

**Who can use this:** Assigned teacher or authorized supervisor

Open a resource, bring an existing tab forward once, or apply a separate browsing restriction.

1. **Open a new destination.** Choose Open URL, review the recipients and address, then send. Classroom assignments lets you choose a connected course, assignment or material, and one link, then Open assignment. Both actions open the chosen link; existing restrictions still apply.
2. **Inspect current tabs.** Open Manage Tabs from the toolbar, or View Tabs for one student. Review the current rows and their availability. Duplicate addresses are separate tabs; an outdated row may require fresh tab information.
3. **Bring Forward once.** When supported, Bring Forward activates that exact existing tab once. It neither restricts browsing nor repeatedly returns the student to the tab. A disabled action explains the connection or extension support it needs.
4. **Set or stop Focus separately.** To restrict browsing, check the intended students and choose Set Focus in the Dashboard toolbar. Choose their current websites or review a specific destination and scope. Stop Focus removes Focus while preserving Flight Paths, Block Lists, Attention, and tab limits. These controls are separate from Manage Tabs and Bring Forward.
5. **Close the intended tabs.** Select exact tab rows and use the available close action. Close All Tabs (bulk) has a broader effect; use it only after checking its recipients. Read the result because a stale or unsupported row may not close.
6. **Apply or clear a tab limit.** Manage Tabs starts with your personal default, then the school default, or unlimited. Enter a limit from 1 to 100 and apply it explicitly. Clear limit removes that limit. Tab-count protection preserves a usable destination and supported sign-in flows.

### Use Focus and Flight Paths

**Who can use this:** Assigned teacher or authorized supervisor

Review the allowed website or resource scope before applying a browsing restriction.

1. **Focus current websites.** Check the recipients and choose Set Focus → Each student's current website. Each student stays within their own current website, which may differ across the selection. This needs fresh online page information. The tile's open padlock is a shortcut for that student's current website.
2. **Review a specific destination.** For a shared destination, enter or choose a domain or URL. Entire website allows browsing across the site; the full URL is a landing page, not an exact-page boundary. Review any listed additional websites or broader-access warnings before sending.
3. **Use precise resources when available.** This resource only appears when precise scope support is enabled. Review the actual boundary: a supported document, form, or video is an item; a Section includes the normalized path and paths below it. A review sends no command. Unsupported recipients keep their existing restriction; ClassPilot does not widen a precise request to the whole website.
4. **Apply a saved Flight Path.** Choose the saved path and review its websites and resources. A Flight Path allows a prepared set of destinations. School policy and teacher Block Lists can still limit access. Sign-in support depends on the school's configuration and the student's compatible client; if access fails, read the outcome and ask an administrator to review it.
5. **Check an automatic preset.** A scheduled class's automatic Flight Path shows its status above the tiles. Review all outcome counts. Turn off for this session stops that preset for the entire named class session, including offline students, and stays off after refresh. It can run again next session if still configured; separate manual overrides are preserved.
6. **Stop Focus and confirm.** Select students and choose Stop Focus, or use an eligible tile's closed padlock. It preserves Flight Paths, Block Lists, Attention, and tab limits. Await browser confirmation. Stop Focus not queued means reconnect or update ClassPilot and retry; it does not promise later removal. Remove Flight Path and Turn off for this session remain separate actions.

> **Check before continuing:** Signed-out students do not have a current page. Read skipped or not-delivered results and retry after sign-in; do not assume a restriction will apply later.

### Use attention and communication tools

**Who can use this:** Assigned teacher or authorized supervisor with supported classroom tools

Choose a Block List, Attention, or a message for the immediate classroom need.

1. **Block a defined set of sites.** Apply a saved Block List from the Dashboard and review the recipients and results. Remove the teacher-applied Block List separately when it is no longer needed. School policy remains in effect.
2. **Use Attention briefly.** In Class tools → Tools, choose Eyes up and review the recipients and message. Attention takes precedence over browsing exceptions. Choose Release attention when students should continue.
3. **Use the active context.** Class tools provides Messages, Help, quick polls, and a timer for your owned class or eligible supervision assignment. My room can provide the same supported tools. Observe remains read-only, and unavailable or incompatible clients may not receive an action.
4. **Clear the right control.** Stop Focus, Remove Flight Path, Remove Block List, Release attention, and Clear limit act independently. Check the outcome of the specific control you want to remove instead of assuming one clears the others.

### Use Class tools during a lesson

**Who can use this:** Assigned teacher or authorized supervisor with classroom tools

Open Help, Messages, Activities, and Tools for your current class or eligible supervision assignment.

1. **Open and arrange the panel.** Choose Class tools at the bottom right. Help and Messages have separate counts, and an active countdown stays visible. Pin the panel beside the tiles, move or resize it, or use Reset position. On smaller screens it becomes a drawer.
2. **Respond to raised hands.** Help shows waiting requests oldest first. Use Student help requests to control whether students can raise a hand. Choose Mark helped when you have finished helping. These requests are visible only to authorized staff.
3. **Find a student to message.** Messages lists the class roster even when the tile grid is filtered. Find a student searches names; Enter opens the conversation when one eligible student matches, and Esc clears the search. Each row shows presence, unread count, and at most one status such as Absent, Not signed in, or With the supervising staff member.
4. **Read and reply privately.** Open a student row, tile Message button, or unread badge. Read the connection and reply-availability explanation before sending. N need reply selects the oldest waiting conversation you can answer. Students with another supervisor and Other conversations may be readable without allowing a reply. An eligible queued message can wait for reconnection within its authorized class.
5. **Choose announcement recipients.** Announce to class targets the reporting students reviewed by the dialog. With checkboxes or a subgroup, the button names that audience instead. If it says Selection cleared · choose students again, rechoose the recipients. The roster list hides message text, but open conversations and incoming alerts can show student messages.
6. **Manage messaging availability.** Pause stops new student messages while allowing teacher replies. Turning class messaging off makes existing conversations read-only; school messaging policy can also do this. Turning class messaging on cannot override school policy. Drafts stay when you switch tools, but a real classroom or account change clears outgoing drafts. Clear thread and End chat for student, when available, are separate actions.
7. **Run a quick poll.** In Activities, choose a quick check or Create poll. Review the question, choices, and exact recipients before sending. Use View responses for the active poll and close it when finished. Only one poll is active in the context, and a student's first accepted answer is retained.
8. **Use a timer or Attention.** In Tools, use Start timer for a countdown of 1–60 minutes and Stop timer when finished. Eyes up and Release attention control Attention. Active tools retain their original recipients when you change the Dashboard selection; review pending, unavailable, failed, and expired delivery outcomes.

> **Flight note:** Open URL, Manage Tabs, Set Focus, Flight Paths, Block Lists, and Student Sign Out remain in the Dashboard toolbar.

> **Flight note:** Extension update required means the selected action needs support the student's current client has not established.

## Monitor



### Use the student drawer responsibly

**Who can use this:** Authorized teacher or administrator

Review the student's current context and the history you are authorized to see.

1. **Open Details.** Choose Details on the intended student's tile. Screens shows current context; Timeline and History provide their available activity views. Clicking the tile's preview opens the enlarged still image instead.
2. **Check time and ownership.** Compare capture or observation times, the active class or supervisor, attendance or pass context, and the connection label. Historical access is limited to authorized teaching and supervision intervals; selecting a date does not grant access to the student's whole day.
3. **Treat missing evidence accurately.** An access error, lost monitoring, an unavailable preview, and an empty history are different states. Use the displayed retry action for a failed read. Do not treat a missing observation as proof that a student stayed on task.
4. **Review with an educational purpose.** Use monitoring for instruction, safety, and support under school policy. A title, category, or screenshot can be incomplete; confirm the context before intervening, and close the drawer when the review is finished.

### Use My room and temporary supervision

**Who can use this:** Staff member with supervision permission or an active assignment

Claim authorized students into My room, review handoffs, and end the correct assignment.

1. **Find the right workspace.** Supervision → Live now lists current sessions; Scheduled lists your eligible dated testing and coverage; Saved groups holds reusable rosters. Saved membership or Authorized staff permission does not start a session or make a student part of active testing.
2. **Find available students.** On the Dashboard, Available starts with All classes and All grades. Combine class, grade, and student search. Class sections describe official roster membership, not which class is meeting now. A student can appear in several sections but is selected and counted once.
3. **Review a Claim into My room.** Select shown adds filtered students to your selection tray. Claim shown reviews only those displayed; Claim selected reviews the full tray, including hidden selections. Review the students, room name, supervisor, and end time, then confirm Claim into my room or Add to my room. Adding students keeps the existing room deadline. A changed availability review may remove students; read the notice.
4. **Keep the room across bells.** My room is your one active personal room across grades and classes. Its students stay with you across scheduled class changes, and the chosen room view survives reloads. Choosing Class or Observe changes the view without transferring room membership. Students leave through removal, expiry, or an explicit handoff.
5. **Recognize earlier Claimed sessions.** Claimed students identifies earlier ad hoc supervision contexts. In Supervision → Live now, Combine my supervision reviews personally held students before moving them into My room. It cannot take another supervisor's students; that supervisor must explicitly Send them.
6. **Use the room's Dashboard tools.** Wait for the room roster to load, then use its screenshots and supported classroom tools, including messages, Help, polls, timers, tabs, and restrictions. Eligible scheduled and ad hoc supervision can provide the full tool set; each student's current authority and client support still apply. In My room, filters narrow the shown students; Set Focus and Stop Focus require checkboxes.
7. **Send to an authorized destination.** Use Send to and review the captured students. Choose a receiving personal room or an authorized group supervisor; for a group, choose an existing session or Create a new session. Review destination, purpose, deadline, current ownership, and eligibility, then Review and Send students. Existing destinations keep their deadline. Continue with N available students requires an explicit new review. The receiving staff member does not need to accept the transfer.
8. **Remove students or end the room.** Remove from room acts on one student; Remove selected acts on the checked room students. To release everyone, including offline and filtered-out members, choose End room and confirm End room and release all. If the roster changes, close the confirmation and review again. Removal restores an eligible current class or returns the student to Available; the browser receives its new state on reconnect.
9. **Use legacy release controls carefully.** In the earlier Claimed students view, Release student acts on one student and Release all acts on its whole loaded roster, including students hidden by search. Those Dashboard actions release immediately. Supervision → Live now reviews a named session's release or end action. Saved group membership remains unchanged.
10. **Review deadlines and testing.** Change end time reviews everyone in an unscheduled session before saving. Adding or sending students never extends an existing deadline. Scheduled testing follows its scheduled end; End testing reviews an early end of that testing assignment without ending an underlying regular class. Start session defaults to Supervision; choose Testing explicitly when appropriate.
11. **Read counts and summaries in context.** The My room badge counts students; the Supervision badge counts visible active sessions. An em dash means a count is unavailable, not zero. After a testing or coverage assignment ends, its activity email covers the students and intervals you actually supervised. Handoffs produce separate staff summaries, and partial releases do not create a new summary email.

> **Flight note:** Activity summaries need a short observation-settlement period, processing, and normal email delivery time. Missing observations do not establish on-task activity; your school may send a central copy.

## Wrap Up



### Wrap up and troubleshoot

**Who can use this:** Teacher or administrator

Close the correct session, review its summary, and report unresolved access or delivery issues.

1. **End the correct assignment.** Scheduled classes end at their assigned time. To finish early, choose End Class and review End Early & Email Summary; manual classes use End Class & Email Summary. Monitoring and that session's controls stop, and a short settlement period precedes the summary email. My room has its own End room action and stays active across class bells until ended or expired.
2. **Review Past sessions.** Use Class actions → Past sessions for recent teaching sessions and their Session Summaries. Read monitoring coverage, gaps, and unavailable evidence before drawing conclusions. Logging out alone does not end a running class or send its summary.
3. **Match the retry to the problem.** For a read or access error, use Refresh class or Refresh coverage. Resync Class reconciles an active manual class roster and is unavailable for a frozen scheduled roster. For a failed student action, address the displayed sign-in, connection, ownership, or compatibility issue, then retry only the affected students.
4. **Ask for administrator help.** Report unresolved extension updates, school blocks, sign-in/provider policy, managed configuration, or official roster and schedule issues. Include the displayed student status, action, and approximate time. Do not send passwords or sign-in URLs. SchoolPilot's web and API releases do not install an extension update on student Chromebooks.
